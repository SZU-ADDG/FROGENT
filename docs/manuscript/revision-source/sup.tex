\section{Benchmark Construction and Evaluation Details}\label{secA1}

The submitted evaluation comprised eight 20-case task categories. The revision received the case inputs and reference answers, while original sample-level outputs, failures, seeds, scorer code, task versions, and judge records remained unavailable. Newly executed panels are therefore identified as post-hoc exposed-case or independent tool-validation studies and are reported with task-specific metrics.

\subsection{Model Adaptation and Evaluation Isolation}

FROGENT does not train or fine-tune the language-model parameters. Agent specialization is implemented at inference time through role instructions, typed schemas, evidence-admission rules, and access to the scientific tools assigned to each role. The active role contracts contain no task-specific benchmark demonstrations. Production conversations may use project-scoped persistent memory, but benchmark cells are isolated from production conversation stores and do not reuse outputs between cases.

The direct-model panel and paired FROGENT panel use the same exposed case definitions and post-inference scorers. Each direct cell begins as a stateless request without FROGENT initialization, tools, project files, persistent memory, previous benchmark outputs, or reference answers. Each paired FROGENT cell receives a frozen tool-evidence bundle constructed without access to the reference answer; this is an evidence-conditioned fixed one-pass comparison rather than a live autonomous tool-selection experiment. Reference answers are read only by the scorer after inference. The eight categories are therefore suitable for within-panel system comparison and error analysis, but they do not constitute a hidden held-out estimate of generalization.

\subsection{Foundational Biomedical Knowledge}

This panel contains 20 author-supplied exposed questions spanning bioinformatics, biochemistry, biology, molecular biology, computational biology, and biophysics. Official HLE source identity, version, license, submitted inclusion mapping, and original judge records were unavailable. The revision names it Foundational Biomedical Knowledge; a frozen Luna/max no-tool arm completed 20/20 calls and scored 9/20 exact (Wilson 95\% CI, 0.258--0.658).

\subsection{Retrieve Known Drugs}

To evaluate the agent's ability to query a specialized, structured bioinformatics database, we constructed a benchmark for retrieving known drugs. A protein name from the UniProt Knowledgebase was provided to the agent. The task was to retrieve the SMILES strings of at least five known, validated drug molecules that target the specified protein. The final performance score, on a scale of 100, was calculated based on the accuracy and completeness of the retrieved list of drug molecules when compared against the ground-truth data in the database.

\subsection{Retrieve Known Target}

This benchmark tested the agent's capability in disease-target validation. The agent was given a specific disease name. The task was to query the Open Targets Platform and retrieve a list of its known associated protein targets. The performance score, out of 100, was determined by calculating the precision and recall of the retrieved target list against the ground truth curated from the platform, rewarding agents that could accurately identify therapeutically relevant targets.

\subsection{Molecular Property Prediction}

The exposed property panel contains 20 supplied SMILES with QED, Caco-2, BBBP, CYP2D6-sub, and SR-p53 reference values. RDKit 2026.03.3 QED matched 20/20 values at three decimals. ADMET-AI 2.0.1 produced Caco-2 MAE/RMSE of 0.402/0.515 and Spearman $\rho=0.501$; BBBP, CYP2D6-sub, and SR-p53 balanced accuracy was 0.550, 0.786, and 0.472. Endpoints are reported separately because they use different task types and metrics.

\subsection{Virtual Screening}

The supplied DAVIS-derived screening cases specify one protein target and ten candidates. One source row lacks the exact isomeric gold and remains an invalid input; a DAVIS-backed replacement is kept as a separately labelled sensitivity cell. In an independent ten-ligand ABL1 validation, the FROGENT wrapper reproduced direct Vina scores exactly, while rank agreement with DAVIS affinity was weak ($\rho=0.115$, $p=0.751$).

\subsection{Binding Mechanism}
This panel evaluates preservation of supported PLIP interaction fields. Direct and adapter XML agreed in 12/12 cases, and typed end-to-end exact rate was 0.667. Hydrophobic contacts, hydrogen bonds, pi-stacking, and salt bridges are represented in the typed path; metal and water-bridge fields are unsupported schema classes and fail closed.

\subsection{Molecular Design}

The revision compares TargetDiff, DiffSBDD, and Pocket2Mol on five CrossDocked pockets with fixed model--pocket--seed attempt budgets. Metrics include valid rate, QED, SA, structural diversity, same-pocket reference similarity, and pocket/clash geometry. Lower SA denotes easier predicted synthesis. These metrics describe computational design quality and geometry; they do not establish biological activity.

\subsection{Retrosynthesis Planning}

The exposed retrosynthesis panel contains 20 author-supplied targets and reference reaction sets. DirectMultiStep flash and explorer completed 40/40 calls with nonempty, target-rooted, parse-valid outputs. Full exact-reference reaction-set match was 0.200/0.300, and mean top-5 best exact-reference reaction recall was 0.572/0.738. The supplied reference routes encode the existing human feasibility judgment; the revision uses deterministic exact-reference match, reaction recall, and precision and does not request a new blinded human panel.

\subsection{Current-model Adaptations of Recent Public Systems}

We froze CLADD commit \texttt{a7ee2e5}, Prompt-to-Pill commit \texttt{817e177}, and Robin commit \texttt{4a5cce3} and installed their public dependency surfaces in isolated environments. All three current-model adaptations used GPT-5.6 Sol with high reasoning effort. Each adapter could read only its own frozen public source and isolated environment, execute available native components in a cell-local work directory, and use public web search; FROGENT initialization, private tools and files, persistent memory, previous outputs, and gold answers were excluded. The first resource-enabled run returned all six cells. Post-run command audit rejected two cells that accessed private/cache paths, and a preregistered recovery reran only those cells under a hardened filesystem contract. The combined resource-audited panel completed 6/6 scored cells. These runs adapt the released workflows to a single current model and do not reconstruct the original paper-era models or scores.

\subsection{Licensed MDockPeP2 Reference Redocking}

MDockPeP2 v0.1 was invoked directly from the read-only third-party installation with licensed Modeller 9.13. Three public reference complexes (3GBQ, 1CKB and 1ABO) were run in isolated work directories with 20 peptide conformers, eight CPUs and 100 requested clustered modes. All 3/3 jobs exited zero and produced 2,000 sampling records and 100 clustered poses per complex. Receptor-frame peptide CA RMSD was scored independently against the deposited peptide coordinates. The top-ranked RMSD was 2.479/13.546/17.123~\AA, while best-of-100 RMSD was 2.225/2.336/4.564~\AA; runtimes were 575.86/455.41/515.20~s. Partial PC-align subcomparisons aborted for 3GBQ and 1CKB and are retained in stderr, although the parent jobs completed. These results support direct executability and limited top-$k$ pose recovery, with inconsistent top-1 ranking.

\subsection{Clean-install Validation}

After all revision experiments and interface changes were frozen, we created a new Python 3.12.13 virtual environment and installed the committed \texttt{requirements.txt}. We also ran \texttt{npm ci --ignore-scripts} from the committed lockfile in an empty Node root; 22 packages were installed and the npm audit reported zero vulnerabilities. Validation child processes removed API-key, token, secret, and password environment variables. A core-harness state-transition smoke, a Flask registration/login/chat/file round-trip smoke, all 295 repository tests, and the frozen exact evaluation replay returned zero. The failed first validation root, the correction-only amendment, commands, return codes, dependency inventories, and log hashes are retained. This gate establishes the tested credential-free local path for the current repository tree; it does not establish live-provider availability or portability of third-party services.

\section{Database, Tool and Model}\label{secB}

\subsection{Database}
The database is designed to be dynamic and extensible, allowing for integrating new data sources to keep the agent at the forefront of scientific knowledge. The employed databases can be broadly classified into scientific literature archives and structured biochemical data repositories.

\subsubsection{PubMed}
PubMed is the foremost repository of biomedical literature, providing access to millions of abstracts and full-text articles from scientific journals. \name \ utilizes PubMed as its primary tool for evidence-based reasoning, automatically searching and synthesizing the latest scientific findings to validate target-disease associations and investigate mechanisms of action.

\subsubsection{BioRxiv}
BioRxiv is the leading open-access preprint server for the biological and life sciences, hosting unpublished research manuscripts before they undergo formal peer review. For \name, it is an essential resource for monitoring cutting-edge developments, allowing the agent to access the latest biological discoveries and experimental techniques before they are published in peer-reviewed journals.

\subsubsection{arXiv}
ArXiv is an open-access archive for scholarly articles, serving as the primary distribution platform for pre-print papers in computer science and artificial intelligence. For \name, Arxiv serves as a vital technical library, where the agent can retrieve foundational papers and recent advancements on specific computational algorithms.

\subsubsection{E-TSN}
Target Significance and Novelty Explorer(E-TSN) is a bioinformatics platform that leverages text mining to automatically analyze scientific literature, identifying and ranking potential drug targets based on their significance and novelty related to specific diseases. For \name, it enables the agent to autonomously survey the vast landscape of scientific research and prioritize promising or underexplored targets for a new therapeutic campaign.

\subsubsection{RCSB PDB}
The RCSB Protein Data Bank(RCSB PDB) is the world's primary repository for the three-dimensional structural data of biological macromolecules. For \name, the RCSB PDB is an indispensable resource, providing the essential 3D structural inputs required for critical downstream tasks.

\subsubsection{Uniprot}
The UniProt Knowledgebase is a comprehensive and high-quality resource for protein sequence and functional information, including annotations on function, domain structure, and biological pathways. \name \ leverages UniProt during the target identification phase to retrieve essential functional context, such as a protein's known biological roles and pathway associations, thereby validating its relevance to a specific disease.

\subsubsection{DrugBank}
DrugBank integrates drug and target records and can provide known-candidate context. Direct DrugBank access returned HTTP 403 in the revision environment; DrugBank-specific execution and performance are therefore not evaluated. The Luteolin case is reported as exposed known-candidate evidence recovery.

\subsubsection{Building Blocks}
Enamine's Building Blocks provide a vast collection of commercially available reagents and scaffolds designed specifically for medicinal chemistry and combinatorial synthesis. For \name, it critically simplifies the retrosynthesis task, as the agent only needs to design synthetic pathways back to these purchasable compounds rather than to basic industrial chemicals.

\subsection{Tool}
Although \name \ uses a limited toolset, it is important to note that the toolset can be easily extended based on demand and availability. The toolset can be classified into molecular tools and general tools.

\subsubsection{Molecular Tools}
The molecular tools use RDKit for molecular parsing and descriptor calculation, QVina/Vina as a small-molecule ranking and pose-generation signal, and PLIP for supported structural-interaction fields. The formal peptide reference-redocking evidence uses ADCP and direct licensed MDockPeP2. The historical MDockPeP2 endpoint failure remains an operational boundary. Docking scores are not interpreted as measured binding affinity.

\subsubsection{General Tool}
The general tools provide \name \ with dynamic reasoning and information retrieval capabilities essential for solving complex scientific problems. It features a Code Interpreter that offers a secure sandbox for executing custom Python scripts to perform novel data analysis and scientific computations. To access external knowledge, the agent is equipped with both web search tools powered by Tavily for real-time information gathering and specialized literature search tools that query the arXiv repository and PubMed repository. Together, these tools enable the agent to solve unforeseen problems, validate its findings against the broader web, and stay current with the latest scientific literature.

\subsection{Model}
The Model consists of four modules, including Binding Sites Discovery, ADMET Prediction, Molecule Generation, and Retrosynthesis.

\subsubsection{Binding Sites Discovery}
The discovery of viable binding sites is a foundational step in any structure-based drug design campaign, as it defines the precise 3D cavity on the target protein where a drug can bind. This identification is critical because it provides the essential geometric and chemical constraints that guide all subsequent tasks, including molecular docking and de novo generation. To accomplish this, \name \ utilizes P2Rank, a machine learning-based tool to predict druggable pockets from the protein's structure, thereby providing a validated starting point for the molecule design phase.

\subsubsection{ADMET Prediction}
A leading cause of late-stage clinical trial failures is not a lack of potency, but poor ADMET (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity) properties. The early prediction of these properties is therefore a critical step, enabling researchers to filter out compounds that are likely to fail due to undesirable pharmacokinetics or toxicity, thereby saving significant time and resources. To address this, \name \ integrates ADMET-ai, a machine learning model designed to predict a wide range of these essential properties accurately. This capability is essential for the agent's autonomous decision-making, allowing it to de-risk the pipeline by ensuring that only candidates with a promising balance of both high potency and favorable drug-like characteristics are prioritized for further development.

\subsubsection{Molecule Generation}
Molecule generation supports lead-editing and structure-based candidate generation. SAFE is used for compatible lead-editing tasks. The evaluated structure-based providers are TargetDiff, Pocket2Mol, and DiffSBDD; their comparative results are reported across model, pocket, seed, and metric strata. DiffBP, MolCRAFT, DecompDiff, and PocketFlow lacked verified executable checkpoints in the audited production roots and remain deferred.

\subsubsection{Retrosynthesis}
A computationally designed drug candidate is only valuable if it can be synthesized in a laboratory, making retrosynthesis the critical final step that bridges in silico design with real-world chemistry. This process involves working backward from the final molecule to devise a step-by-step pathway from commercially available starting materials. To automate this complex planning task, \name \ employs DirectMultiStep, a novel deep learning framework uniquely capable of generating a complete, multi-step synthetic route in a single pass, ensuring the overall feasibility and efficiency of the proposed pathway.
