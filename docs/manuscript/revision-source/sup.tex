\section{Benchmark Construction and Evaluation Details}\label{secA1}

The submitted evaluation comprised eight 20-case task categories. The revision received the case inputs and reference answers, while original sample-level outputs, failures, seeds, scorer code, task versions, and judge records remained unavailable. Newly executed panels are therefore identified as post-hoc exposed-case or independent tool-validation studies and are reported with task-specific metrics.

\subsection{Model Adaptation and Evaluation Isolation}

FROGENT does not train or fine-tune the language-model parameters. Agent specialization is implemented at inference time through role instructions, typed schemas, evidence-admission rules, and access to the scientific tools assigned to each role. The active role contracts contain no task-specific benchmark demonstrations. Production conversations may use project-scoped persistent memory, but benchmark cells are isolated from production conversation stores and do not reuse outputs between cases.

The direct-model panel and paired FROGENT panel use the same exposed case definitions and post-inference scorers. Each direct cell begins as a stateless request without FROGENT initialization, tools, project files, persistent memory, previous benchmark outputs, or reference answers. Each paired FROGENT cell receives a frozen tool-evidence bundle constructed without access to the reference answer; this is an evidence-conditioned fixed one-pass comparison rather than a live autonomous tool-selection experiment. Reference answers are read only by the scorer after inference. The eight categories are therefore suitable for within-panel system comparison and error analysis, but they do not constitute a hidden held-out estimate of generalization.

\subsection{Agent Role Contracts and Scientific Action Space}

The role prompts define scientific responsibilities, allowed actions, output schemas and stopping conditions. They contain no benchmark examples. The Orchestrate Agent maintains the campaign state and delegates scientific operations; Retrieve admits versioned evidence; Forge proposes structures and routes; Gauge returns task-specific measurements and ranked feedback. Tool observations enter the shared working context only after schema and provenance checks. A later conflict or retraction can revoke an admitted observation before final synthesis. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:sup-agent-contracts} gives the manuscript-scope action space rather than an exhaustive inventory of every optional connector.

\subsection{Foundational Biomedical Knowledge}

This panel contains 20 author-supplied exposed questions spanning bioinformatics, biochemistry, biology, molecular biology, computational biology, and biophysics. Official HLE source identity, version, license, submitted inclusion mapping, and original judge records were unavailable. The revision names it Foundational Biomedical Knowledge; a frozen Luna/max no-tool arm completed 20/20 calls and scored 9/20 exact (Wilson 95\% CI, 0.258--0.658).

\subsection{Retrieve Known Drugs}

To evaluate the agent's ability to query a specialized, structured bioinformatics database, we constructed a benchmark for retrieving known drugs. A protein name from the UniProt Knowledgebase was provided to the agent. The task was to retrieve the SMILES strings of at least five known, validated drug molecules that target the specified protein. The final performance score, on a scale of 100, was calculated based on the accuracy and completeness of the retrieved list of drug molecules when compared against the ground-truth data in the database.

\subsection{Retrieve Known Target}

This benchmark tested the agent's capability in disease-target validation. The agent was given a specific disease name. The task was to query the Open Targets Platform and retrieve a list of its known associated protein targets. The performance score, out of 100, was determined by calculating the precision and recall of the retrieved target list against the ground truth curated from the platform, rewarding agents that could accurately identify therapeutically relevant targets.

\subsection{Molecular Property Prediction}

The exposed property panel contains 20 supplied SMILES with QED, Caco-2, BBBP, CYP2D6-sub, and SR-p53 reference values. RDKit 2026.03.3 QED matched 20/20 values at three decimals. ADMET-AI 2.0.1 produced Caco-2 MAE/RMSE of 0.402/0.515 and Spearman $\rho=0.501$; BBBP, CYP2D6-sub, and SR-p53 balanced accuracy was 0.550, 0.786, and 0.472. Endpoints are reported separately because they use different task types and metrics.

\subsection{Virtual Screening}

The supplied DAVIS-derived screening cases specify one protein target and ten candidates. One source row lacks the exact isomeric gold and remains an invalid input; a DAVIS-backed replacement is kept as a separately labelled sensitivity cell. In an independent ten-ligand ABL1 validation, the FROGENT wrapper reproduced direct Vina scores exactly, while rank agreement with DAVIS affinity was weak ($\rho=0.115$, $p=0.751$).

\subsection{Binding Mechanism}
This panel evaluates preservation of supported PLIP interaction fields. Direct and adapter XML agreed in 12/12 cases, and typed end-to-end exact rate was 0.667. Hydrophobic contacts, hydrogen bonds, pi-stacking, and salt bridges are represented in the typed path; metal and water-bridge fields are unsupported schema classes and fail closed.

\subsection{Molecular Design}

The revision compares TargetDiff, DiffSBDD, and Pocket2Mol on five CrossDocked pockets with fixed model--pocket--seed attempt budgets. Metrics include valid rate, QED, SA, structural diversity, same-pocket reference similarity, and pocket/clash geometry. Lower SA denotes easier predicted synthesis. These metrics describe computational design quality and geometry; they do not establish biological activity.

\subsubsection{Generation stability and structural novelty}

The primary three-seed matrix, independent three-seed extension and pooled six-seed matrix completed 45/45, 45/45 and 90/90 model--pocket--seed jobs. TargetDiff retained the highest valid rate and QED in each analysis, while Pocket2Mol retained the most favorable synthetic-accessibility score. Primary-to-pooled model--pocket rank correlations were 0.993 for valid rate, 0.971 for QED and 0.704 for synthetic accessibility. TargetDiff QED changed from 0.458 to 0.408 and valid rate from 0.968 to 0.949; the corresponding extension-minus-primary pocket-cluster 95\% CIs were $[-0.188,-0.004]$ and $[-0.073,-0.006]$. The stable model order therefore coexisted with seed-sensitive absolute values.

We next tested whether generated molecules reproduced the reference ligand, target-matched known actives or the declared training corpus. The primary analysis parsed 9,767 of 9,825 SDFs. No parsed molecule had exact identity to its same-pocket reference ligand, the reference scaffold or ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity of at least 0.5. Against ChEMBL 37 target-matched strong actives, every nearest-neighbour similarity was below 0.5 and exact active-scaffold overlap occurred in 3/9,767 molecules. Against a versioned CrossDocked training proxy containing 10,404 unique canonical molecules, no generated molecule had exact identity; one DiffSBDD molecule reached similarity of at least 0.5 and none reached 0.7. Scaffold reuse was model dependent (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:sup-generation-novelty}).

\subsubsection{Matched-budget Forge--Gauge feedback allocation}

We separated generator quality from allocation policy in five pockets and three prospectively selected seeds. Every condition received 1,500 attempts per pocket--seed cell. Fixed-best assigned all attempts to TargetDiff; uniform single-pass divided attempts equally among TargetDiff, DiffSBDD and Pocket2Mol; feedback allocation first assigned 250 attempts to each model and used validity, QED and favorable synthetic accessibility to allocate the remaining 750 attempts. Gauge selected TargetDiff in all 15 cells. Feedback allocation improved valid rate and top-50 selection score relative to uniform allocation (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:sup-forge-gauge}). Fixed-best retained the highest valid rate, and the feedback-versus-fixed top-50 difference lacked statistical support. Geometry analysis completed 45/45 cells and 2,250 selected candidates. Pocket compatibility was 1.000 in all conditions, and severe-clash-free rates were 0.9987, 1.0000 and 0.9987 for fixed-best, uniform and feedback allocation, respectively.

\subsection{Retrosynthesis Planning}

The exposed retrosynthesis panel contains 20 author-supplied targets and reference reaction sets. DirectMultiStep flash and explorer completed 40/40 calls with nonempty, target-rooted, parse-valid outputs. Full exact-reference reaction-set match was 0.200/0.300, and mean top-5 best exact-reference reaction recall was 0.572/0.738. The supplied reference routes encode the existing human feasibility judgment; the revision uses deterministic exact-reference match, reaction recall, and precision and does not request a new blinded human panel.

\subsection{Licensed MDockPeP2 Reference Redocking}

MDockPeP2 v0.1 was invoked directly from the read-only third-party installation with licensed Modeller 9.13. Three public reference complexes (3GBQ, 1CKB and 1ABO) were run in isolated work directories with 20 peptide conformers, eight CPUs and 100 requested clustered modes. \subsection{Clean-install Validation}

After all revision experiments and interface changes were frozen, we created a new Python 3.12.13 virtual environment and installed the committed \texttt{requirements.txt}. \section{Data Resources and Executable Scientific Providers}\label{secB}

\subsection{Literature and structured data resources}

The retrieval layer distinguishes literature records from structured biochemical records. PubMed and Europe PMC provide biomedical citations and accessible abstracts or full text; bioRxiv supplies biology preprints; arXiv supplies computational-method preprints. Literature records retain title, authorship, year, persistent identifier, retrieval source and the passage used by the synthesis step. Preprints remain labelled separately from peer-reviewed articles.

UniProt supplies protein identity, sequence, reviewed annotations and database cross-references. Open Targets supplies disease--target associations. RCSB PDB supplies deposited macromolecular coordinates, ligand identifiers and structure metadata. E-TSN supplies literature-derived target-significance and novelty context when configured. Enamine Building Blocks provides a purchasable starting-material constraint for synthesis planning. DrugBank integrates drug and target records, although direct access returned HTTP 403 in the revision environment; DrugBank-specific execution is excluded from the measured provider results, and the Luteolin case is treated as known-candidate evidence recovery.

\subsection{Molecular calculation and structural analysis}

RDKit provides molecular parsing, canonicalization, fingerprints, scaffold extraction and deterministic descriptors. ADMET-AI supplies learned property estimates for the endpoint-specific panel. P2Rank proposes protein pockets. Vina supplies small-molecule poses and ranking scores, and PLIP converts protein--ligand complexes into supported typed interaction records. The direct-versus-wrapper and reference-pose experiments define the interpretation of these outputs: Vina is used as a docking signal rather than an affinity measurement, and unsupported PLIP schema classes fail closed.

ADCP and licensed MDockPeP2 provide peptide-docking poses. ESMFold and AlphaFold 3 provide sequence-to-complex structural predictions. These providers are evaluated independently because a predicted complex, a docked pose and a confidence score represent different scientific objects. Their coordinate-grounded results are reported in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:sup-peptide-comparators}.

\subsection{Candidate generation and synthesis planning}

SAFE supports compatible lead-editing tasks. TargetDiff, Pocket2Mol and DiffSBDD form the executable structure-based generation portfolio evaluated across models, pockets, seeds and metrics. DiffBP, MolCRAFT, DecompDiff and PocketFlow lacked verified executable checkpoints in the audited production roots and remain outside the active portfolio. DirectMultiStep produces candidate multistep retrosynthesis routes. Route evaluation reports target rooting, molecular parse validity and overlap with the supplied human-selected reference reactions; an exact mismatch is not used as evidence that an alternative route is chemically impossible.

\subsection{General computation and web retrieval}

The runtime exposes sandboxed Python execution for task-specific analysis and configured web search for public information retrieval. Literature and web results enter the same provenance and evidence-admission path as structured-provider results. Availability of a connector is recorded separately from successful execution, and provider-dependent claims require a completed versioned call under the reported access conditions.
